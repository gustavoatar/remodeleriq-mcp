## The Headline Numbers: 2026 Kitchen Remodel Costs by Tier

A "kitchen remodel" can mean anything from a cabinet repaint to a wall-removal-plus-everything overhaul. So before we talk price, you need to know which tier you're actually in.

### Minor Kitchen Refresh — $28,000 to $42,000

This is paint, hardware, countertop replacement, maybe a new sink and faucet. Cabinets stay where they are; you might reface them. Appliances stay or get a mid-range upgrade.

What Zonda calls a "Minor Kitchen Remodel" averaged **$26,790 nationally in 2024** and has tracked upward roughly **6.8% per year** since (driven mostly by material costs — see FRED's Producer Price Index for "Construction Materials," which is up ~14% over the same window). That puts the 2026 fair-market range at **$28,000 to $42,000** depending on your metro.

**Where the money goes:**
- Labor: 38–42% of total
- Cabinets/refacing: 22–28%
- Countertops: 12–18%
- Appliances: 10–15%
- Plumbing/electrical (light): 6–10%

### Mid-Range Full Remodel — $58,000 to $89,000

New semi-custom cabinets, quartz or granite counters, new mid-range appliances, new flooring, fresh lighting and electrical, new sink/faucet, paint, hardware. No structural changes.

This is the most common remodel category — and it's where overcharging is most common, because the project is too complex for a homeowner to mentally cost-check on the fly.

National median in 2026: **$72,400.** Range: **$58,000 to $89,000.** Anything above $95,000 in this tier should raise an eyebrow unless you're in a high-cost metro (San Francisco, NYC, Boston) or specifying premium finishes.

### High-End Major Remodel — $115,000 to $185,000+

Wall removal, structural changes, custom cabinetry, professional-grade appliances (Wolf, Sub-Zero, Miele), stone or specialty surfaces, designer lighting, often paired with adjacent room flow changes (opening up to a great room, adding an island, relocating plumbing).

Range varies wildly here because the spec drives everything. If you're in this tier and your quote is over **$200,000 for a kitchen under 250 sqft**, you should be getting a written line-item breakdown — not a single lump-sum number.

## The Three Lines Most Likely to Be Padded

In thousands of bids we've helped homeowners analyze, three line items consistently come back inflated:

### 1. Demolition

A standard kitchen demo (gut to studs, haul away) should run **$1,400 to $2,800** for an average 180–240 sqft kitchen in 2026. If your demo line is over **$4,500** without a clear reason (asbestos, lath-and-plaster, structural surprises), ask for the breakdown.

### 2. Cabinet Installation Labor

Semi-custom cabinet *materials* might be $9,000–$14,000. *Installation* labor on top of that should be **15–22% of cabinet material cost** — so $1,800 to $3,100. Some contractors quietly list installation at 35–40% of material cost. That's not standard.

### 3. "Project Management" / "General Conditions"

This is the most-padded line in the industry. A 5–8% management fee on a $70K remodel = $3,500–$5,600, which is fair for an established GC. If you're seeing 15% ($10,500) labeled as "general conditions" or "project supervision" *on top of* labor that's already marked up, you're double-paying.

## What a Fair 2026 Mid-Range Kitchen Quote Should Look Like

Here's the rough shape of a clean quote for a $72,000 mid-range remodel. Use this as a sanity check:

| Line Item | Range |
|---|---|
| Demolition + disposal | $1,600 – $2,800 |
| Cabinets (semi-custom) | $14,000 – $19,000 |
| Cabinet installation | $2,200 – $3,400 |
| Countertops (quartz, 50 sqft) | $4,200 – $6,800 |
| Appliances (mid-range, 4 pcs) | $7,800 – $11,500 |
| Plumbing rough + fixtures | $3,400 – $5,200 |
| Electrical (lighting, GFCI, 220v) | $2,600 – $4,400 |
| Flooring (luxury vinyl or tile, 200 sqft) | $3,200 – $5,400 |
| Drywall, paint, trim | $2,800 – $4,800 |
| Permits + inspections | $400 – $1,200 |
| General conditions (5–8%) | $3,800 – $5,800 |
| **Total** | **$58,000 – $89,000** |

If your quote sums to roughly this — even with different line names — you're in a fair range. If three or more lines are *significantly* over the top end, you have a negotiation conversation to start.
