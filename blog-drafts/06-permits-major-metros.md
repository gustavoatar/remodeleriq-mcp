The conversation most homeowners never have until it's too late: "Wait, do we need a permit for this?"

The honest answer: probably yes, more than you think. And the permit process varies wildly by metro, by project type, and by whether your contractor is doing what they should.

Here's the working guide for 8 major US metros — what permits cost, how long they take, and the specific traps to avoid.

## When you absolutely need a permit (every metro)

- Any electrical work beyond outlet replacement
- Any plumbing work beyond fixture swap
- Moving any wall (load-bearing or not)
- Roof replacement
- Window or door size changes
- Adding square footage
- Decks over 30" off the ground
- Pool installation
- HVAC system replacement

When you usually don't need a permit (varies by city):
- Paint, flooring, cabinet refacing
- Like-for-like fixture replacement
- Cosmetic-only updates with no electrical/plumbing changes

**Critical rule across all metros:** the contractor pulls the permit, never you. A contractor who asks you to pull the permit yourself is doing it to shift liability — if the work is wrong, the city comes after you, not them.

## Atlanta Metro (Fulton, DeKalb, Cobb, Gwinnett)

**Average permit cost (mid-range remodel):** $400–$1,800

**Typical timeline:** 2–4 weeks from submission to issuance for residential remodels under $100K. Add 2–4 weeks if your project triggers HOA review.

**Atlanta-specific traps:**
- DeKalb County requires sealed engineer drawings for any structural change, even minor. Budget $1,200–$2,400 for engineer cost on top of permit fees.
- City of Atlanta has slower processing than surrounding counties — Cobb and Gwinnett typically beat it by 1–2 weeks.
- Roswell and Alpharetta require energy efficiency compliance verification on any major remodel touching HVAC.

## Phoenix Metro (Maricopa County)

**Average permit cost:** $300–$1,400

**Typical timeline:** 1–3 weeks, expedited reviews available for extra fee.

**Phoenix-specific traps:**
- Pool permits are intensive — barrier compliance inspection is rigorous. Allow 3–6 weeks for pool projects.
- Solar installations require separate permits in addition to general construction permits.
- HOA approval is required in roughly 60% of Maricopa County subdivisions and adds 2–6 weeks.

## Dallas-Fort Worth Metro

**Average permit cost:** $350–$1,500

**Typical timeline:** 2–4 weeks in Dallas proper, 1–2 weeks in suburban municipalities.

**DFW-specific traps:**
- Foundation work requires engineer-stamped plans across most municipalities — clay soil makes structural changes more involved than in other Texas metros.
- Plano and Frisco have moved to online submission — faster but stricter on plan completeness.
- HOA approval requirements vary dramatically between cities. Highland Park and University Park are particularly strict.

## Chicago Metro (Cook County)

**Average permit cost:** $600–$2,800

**Typical timeline:** 4–8 weeks within City of Chicago. 2–4 weeks in most suburbs.

**Chicago-specific traps:**
- Chicago proper has the slowest permit office in the metro. Plan accordingly.
- Lead paint disclosure and RRP (Renovation, Repair, Painting) certification required for pre-1978 homes.
- Some neighborhoods (Lincoln Park, Logan Square, Bucktown) have additional landmark or historic district review adding 4–8 weeks.

## New York Metro (NYC + Westchester + Long Island)

**Average permit cost:** $800–$4,500

**Typical timeline:** 6–16 weeks in NYC. 2–6 weeks in Long Island municipalities. Westchester varies widely.

**NYC-specific traps:**
- DOB filings require licensed expediters for anything beyond minor work. Budget $1,500–$4,000 for expediter fees.
- Co-op and condo buildings have their own pre-permit board approval process — sometimes 2–4 months on top of DOB time.
- Asbestos abatement testing required for any pre-1980 building before demo work can start.

## Los Angeles Metro

**Average permit cost:** $500–$2,500

**Typical timeline:** 4–10 weeks in LA City. 2–4 weeks in surrounding cities.

**LA-specific traps:**
- Seismic compliance is required on any structural change, even minor.
- LA City has Title 24 energy compliance requirements that add complexity to HVAC and window changes.
- Coastal Commission jurisdiction adds significant review time for any project within 1,000 feet of the coast.

## Seattle Metro (King County)

**Average permit cost:** $450–$2,100

**Typical timeline:** 3–6 weeks for most residential remodels.

**Seattle-specific traps:**
- Energy code compliance is among the strictest in the country. Window changes and HVAC work require careful documentation.
- Many neighborhoods have tree preservation ordinances that affect deck and addition projects.
- The "View" review process applies to any change above original roof height.

## Boston Metro

**Average permit cost:** $500–$2,200

**Typical timeline:** 3–8 weeks across most municipalities.

**Boston-specific traps:**
- Historic district designation applies to large portions of Boston, Cambridge, and Brookline — adds 4–10 weeks for exterior changes.
- Many homes pre-1900 — lead paint, knob-and-tube wiring, and asbestos common. Budget for surprises.
- Inspectional Services Department is overloaded — multiple revisit visits common.

## How permit costs affect your bid

A fair bid includes permit fees as a known line item or labeled allowance with a realistic dollar range. If your contractor says *"permits as needed,"* push for:

1. Which specific permits will be pulled
2. Estimated cost of each (or a stated allowance)
3. Who pays if permit costs exceed the allowance
4. Timeline impact on the project schedule

## The single biggest permit mistake

Letting the contractor talk you out of pulling a permit on something that needs one.

The contractor's pitch: *"We don't need a permit for this, it's just a small change."* Their motivation: pulled permits trigger inspections, inspections find issues, issues mean rework, rework eats into their margin.

Your motivation: unpermitted work voids homeowner's insurance, complicates the next home sale (buyers' inspectors find it), and can result in personal fines from the city — assessed to *you*, the homeowner, not the contractor.

**Rule of thumb:** if you're paying $5,000+ for any work involving electrical, plumbing, structural, or roofing, a permit is almost certainly required. Verify with your local building department directly — it takes 5 minutes by phone.
