Things to know in PA

From real homeowner and contractor discussions in your state.

Regional insight

Radon Mitigation: Pennsylvania has some of the highest radon levels in the US—testing is critical The EPA estimates 40% of PA homes have elevated radon. Reddit users strongly recommend testing before any basement renovation. Mitigation systems ($800-1500) are effective and should be installed by certified professionals.

Regional insight

No Statewide Contractor License: Pennsylvania has no state-level general contractor licensing Like Texas, PA doesn

Regional insight

Philadelphia Licensing & Permits: Philadelphia has its own contractor licensing and strict permit requirements Philly requires contractor registration with L&I (Licenses & Inspections). Permits are needed for most work, and the city has been cracking down on unpermitted renovations. Reddit users report permit delays, especially for older rowhomes.

Regional insight

Old Rowhome Challenges: Philadelphia and Pittsburgh rowhomes have shared walls creating unique renovation challenges Party walls mean your renovation affects neighbors. Reddit users report needing neighbor notification, party wall agreements for major work, and discovering surprises like shared beams or ductwork. Historic districts add more restrictions.

Regional insight

Lead Paint & Older Homes: PA has high rates of pre-1978 housing with lead paint PA law requires lead-safe work practices. Philadelphia has strict lead certification requirements for contractors. Reddit users recommend testing before any work that disturbs painted surfaces and using EPA RRP-certified contractors.

Pro Tip: Don't ignore the "lock-in" effect.

With many homeowners improving in place, local contractors are in high demand. If a bid looks too good to be true, get it reviewed before you sign—inspectors may catch code or quality issues later that cost you more.
