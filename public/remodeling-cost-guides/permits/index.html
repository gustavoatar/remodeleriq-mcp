How Much Is a
Building Permit?

$425–$1,650 for a typical mid-size remodel in 2026 — but your city sets the rate. Look up localized fees, rules, and timelines for 152 US metros below.

The short answer

A building permit for a mid-size remodel typically costs $425–$1,650 in 2026, ranging from $350 in low-cost metros to $2,550 for high-value projects in expensive markets. Most US jurisdictions charge a valuation-based fee of roughly 0.5–2% of project value, plus plan-review and per-trade permit minimums.

Permit guides by project

Requirements and fees differ by project type — pick yours, then pick your city.

Kitchen Remodel

Yes, if the work moves plumbing, adds circuits, changes gas lines, or removes walls. A same-layout cosmetic refresh usually needs no permit.

Bathroom Remodel

Yes, when fixtures move or new wiring/venting goes in. Swapping a vanity or retiling in place is usually exempt.

Basement Remodel

Yes — finishing a basement almost always needs building, electrical, and often plumbing permits, plus egress compliance for any bedroom.

Home Addition

Always — an addition is new structure. Expect full plan review, zoning checks, and multiple inspections.

ADU / Garage Conversion

Always — converting space into a dwelling unit triggers full plan review, zoning approval, and utility sign-offs.

Deck

Usually — any deck attached to the house or over about 30 inches off grade needs a permit; low floating decks are often exempt.

Building permit costs: FAQ

How much is a building permit for a remodel?
A building permit for a mid-size remodel typically costs $425–$1,650 in 2026, ranging from $350 in low-cost metros to $2,550 for high-value projects in expensive markets. Most US jurisdictions charge a valuation-based fee of roughly 0.5–2% of project value, plus plan-review and per-trade permit minimums.
What determines building permit cost?
Project valuation drives the fee: most cities charge a percentage (commonly 0.5–2%) of the declared construction value, plus flat plan-review fees and minimums per trade permit (electrical, plumbing, mechanical). Bigger scope and pricier markets mean higher fees.
Does every remodeling project need a permit?
No. Structural work, electrical, plumbing, additions, decks, and roofing require permits almost everywhere. Cosmetic work — painting, flooring, countertops, same-layout cabinet swaps — generally does not. Rules vary by city, so check your local guide below.
Are permit fees included in a contractor's bid?
They should be, as a visible line item. A bid that omits permit fees is understating the real project cost — and a contractor who proposes skipping permits is creating resale and liability problems that land on you, not them.
